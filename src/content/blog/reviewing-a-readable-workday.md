Reviewing the day does not need to become another productivity project. A few deliberate minutes are enough to recover the thread before it disappears.

Start with what changed. Look at the issues you touched, the focus sessions you finished, and the habits or check-ins you marked. Resist the urge to score the day too quickly; first, reconstruct it.

## Three questions

1. What moved forward?
2. What stayed open, and why?
3. What context should be waiting for you tomorrow?

The first question gives the day credit for its actual progress. The second separates a real constraint from a vague feeling of being behind. The third turns reflection into a useful starting point.

## Keep the record small

The best review is one you will repeat. A sentence for each question is usually enough. Over time, those small records become more useful than a perfect weekly summary because they preserve the texture of the work while it is still fresh.
